# Hadith: Classification, Preservation and Compilation

## 1. What is a Hadith?

A **Hadith (حديث)** is a report describing something attributed to the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, such as:

* **His sayings** — what he said
* **His actions** — what he did
* **His approvals** — something done in his presence that he approved or did not reject
* **His descriptions** — his character, appearance, manners, etc.

A hadith normally consists of two main parts:

* **Isnād (الإسناد)** — the chain of people who transmitted the report.
* **Matn (المتن)** — the actual text or content of the report.

---

## 2. Main Categories of Hadith

Hadith scholars developed detailed criteria for evaluating the reliability of reports. The four terms below provide a useful basic classification.

### 🟢 1. Ṣaḥīḥ — صحيح

**Meaning:** Authentic / sound

A **Ṣaḥīḥ** hadith is one whose chain and transmission satisfy the established requirements of authenticity.

Generally, scholars require:

1. **Connected chain (اتصال السند)**
   Every narrator received the hadith from the preceding narrator.

2. **Upright narrators (عدالة الرواة)**
   The narrators are considered trustworthy and morally reliable.

3. **Accurate narrators (ضبط الرواة)**
   The narrators demonstrated reliable memory or accurate preservation of their written material.

4. **No contradiction (عدم الشذوذ)**
   The narration does not contradict a stronger, more reliable narration.

5. **No hidden defect (عدم العلة)**
   There is no subtle defect that undermines its authenticity.

**In simple terms:**

> **Ṣaḥīḥ = a report whose chain and transmission meet the highest standard of accepted authenticity.**

---

### 🟡 2. Ḥasan — حسن

**Meaning:** Good / acceptable

A **Ḥasan** hadith generally fulfills the requirements of an acceptable hadith, but the precision of one or more narrators is somewhat below the level normally required for Ṣaḥīḥ.

The narrators may still be trustworthy, but their **ḍabṭ (precision)** is not as strong as that of the narrators of a Ṣaḥīḥ narration.

**In simple terms:**

> **Ḥasan = reliable and acceptable, but below Ṣaḥīḥ in strength.**

A Ḥasan hadith can be used as evidence in Islamic scholarship, subject to the principles of the relevant field.

---

### 🟠 3. Ḍaʿīf — ضعيف

**Meaning:** Weak

A **Ḍaʿīf** hadith is a report that fails to meet one or more of the conditions required for Ṣaḥīḥ or Ḥasan.

The weakness can have different causes, for example:

* A break in the chain
* An unreliable narrator
* A narrator whose precision is insufficient
* An unidentified narrator
* A serious contradiction with stronger reports
* A hidden defect

**In simple terms:**

> **Ḍaʿīf = a report whose chain or transmission does not meet the requirements of an acceptable hadith.**

Importantly, **Ḍaʿīf does not automatically mean fabricated**. A weak hadith may contain a true statement, but the particular chain of transmission does not establish it reliably as a saying of the Prophet ﷺ.

---

### 🔴 4. Mawḍūʿ — موضوع

**Meaning:** Fabricated / invented

A **Mawḍūʿ** report is one that scholars have determined was **fabricated and falsely attributed to the Prophet ﷺ**.

It is therefore not a genuine Prophetic hadith.

Possible indications of fabrication include:

* A known narrator admitting that he fabricated the report
* Clear evidence that a supposed narrator could not have transmitted it
* Serious historical impossibility
* Evidence of fabrication in the chain
* Content or wording that reveals fabrication according to established scholarly criteria

**In simple terms:**

> **Mawḍūʿ = deliberately fabricated and falsely attributed to the Prophet ﷺ.**

---

## 3. The Basic Difference

| Classification | Arabic | Meaning           | General strength             |
| -------------- | ------ | ----------------- | ---------------------------- |
| 🟢 **Ṣaḥīḥ**   | صحيح   | Authentic / sound | Highest                      |
| 🟡 **Ḥasan**   | حسن    | Good / acceptable | Strong, but below Ṣaḥīḥ      |
| 🟠 **Ḍaʿīf**   | ضعيف   | Weak              | Not established as authentic |
| 🔴 **Mawḍūʿ**  | موضوع  | Fabricated        | False attribution            |

> **Important:** Hadith classification is more sophisticated than simply putting every report into four boxes. Scholars have many additional classifications and degrees of strength.

# 4. How Were Hadith Preserved?

The preservation of Hadith developed over several generations. It was **not a single event in which all hadith were suddenly written down in one book**.

## ① During the Lifetime of the Prophet ﷺ

The Companions learned the Prophet's teachings directly from him.

They preserved them through:

* **Memorization**
* **Repetition and teaching**
* **Practice**
* **Writing**

Although much of the early transmission was oral, **writing hadith also occurred during the Prophet's lifetime**.

Some Companions kept written records of Prophetic teachings.

Therefore, it is inaccurate to say that hadith were completely unwritten until centuries later.

---

## ② The Companions — الصحابة

After the death of the Prophet ﷺ in **11 AH / 632 CE**, the Companions became the primary transmitters of his Sunnah.

They taught the next generation, the **Tābiʿūn (تابعون)**.

Hadith were transmitted through:

* Memorization
* Written notes and collections
* Teaching circles
* Direct transmission from teacher to student
* Travel to scholars to hear particular narrations

As the Muslim world expanded, Companions and their students became distributed across different regions.

---

## ③ The Tābiʿūn and Early Generations

The **Tābiʿūn** learned directly from the Companions and transmitted the knowledge to the next generation.

During this period, scholars increasingly emphasized the importance of identifying **who had transmitted each report**.

This developed into the concept of the:

### Isnād — الإسناد

For example:

> Scholar A ← Scholar B ← Companion ← Prophet ﷺ

The chain allowed scholars to investigate:

* Who transmitted the report?
* Did these people actually meet?
* Were they trustworthy?
* How accurate were they?
* Did other students transmit the same report?
* Were there contradictory versions?

---

# 5. The Development of Hadith Criticism

As the Muslim world expanded and the first generations passed away, scholars developed increasingly sophisticated methods for evaluating reports.

This became known as:

### ʿUlūm al-Ḥadīth — علوم الحديث

**The Sciences of Hadith**

It includes subjects such as:

* **ʿIlm ar-Rijāl (علم الرجال)** — study of narrators
* **Al-Jarḥ wa at-Taʿdīl (الجرح والتعديل)** — criticism and accreditation of narrators
* **ʿIlm al-ʿIlal (علم العلل)** — identification of hidden defects
* **Gharīb al-Ḥadīth (غريب الحديث)** — explanation of unusual words and expressions
* **Mukhtalif al-Ḥadīth (مختلف الحديث)** — dealing with apparently conflicting narrations
* **Nasikh wa Mansukh (الناسخ والمنسوخ)** — identifying abrogation where applicable

The scholars did not examine only the **text**. They also carefully examined the **chain of transmission and the individual narrators**.

---

# 6. Early Written Collections

Hadith writing existed early, but the development of **large, systematic collections** happened gradually.

Among the early important works was:

### 📖 Al-Muwaṭṭaʾ — الموطأ

Compiled by **Imam Mālik ibn Anas** (d. 179 AH / 795 CE).

It contains hadith together with statements and legal opinions of Companions and early scholars.

---

# 7. The Major Compilation Period

During the 2nd and 3rd Islamic centuries, hadith scholarship expanded considerably.

Important works included:

### 📚 Musnad Aḥmad — مسند أحمد

Compiled by **Imam Aḥmad ibn Ḥanbal** (d. 241 AH / 855 CE).

It is arranged primarily according to the **Companions who narrated the reports**.

### 📚 Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī — صحيح البخاري

Compiled by **Imam Muḥammad ibn Ismāʿīl al-Bukhārī** (d. 256 AH / 870 CE).

It is one of the most highly regarded collections of authentic hadith in Sunni Islam.

### 📚 Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim — صحيح مسلم

Compiled by **Imam Muslim ibn al-Ḥajjāj** (d. 261 AH / 875 CE).

It is also one of the most highly regarded collections of authentic hadith.

### 📚 Sunan Abī Dāwūd — سنن أبي داود

Compiled by **Imam Abū Dāwūd** (d. 275 AH / 889 CE).

### 📚 Jāmiʿ at-Tirmidhī — جامع الترمذي

Compiled by **Imam at-Tirmidhī** (d. 279 AH / 892 CE).

It is particularly valuable because Imam at-Tirmidhī frequently discusses the grading and scholarly opinions concerning narrations.

### 📚 Sunan an-Nasāʾī — سنن النسائي

Compiled by **Imam an-Nasāʾī** (d. 303 AH / 915 CE).

### 📚 Sunan Ibn Mājah — سنن ابن ماجه

Compiled by **Imam Ibn Mājah** (d. 273 AH / 887 CE).

---

# 8. The Six Major Sunni Collections

The following six books are commonly known as:

## Kutub as-Sittah — الكتب الستة

1. **Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī**
2. **Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim**
3. **Sunan Abī Dāwūd**
4. **Jāmiʿ at-Tirmidhī**
5. **Sunan an-Nasāʾī**
6. **Sunan Ibn Mājah**

### Important distinction

The six books **do not all have the same purpose or authentication standard**.

In particular:

> **Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim were specifically compiled as collections of authentic hadith according to their authors' criteria.**

The **Sunan** collections contain hadith of different grades, including authentic, acceptable and weak reports.

Therefore:

> **“It is in the Six Books” does NOT automatically mean “it is Ṣaḥīḥ.”**

---

# 9. How Does This Relate to Riyāḍ as-Sāliḥīn?

**Riyāḍ as-Sāliḥīn (رياض الصالحين)** was compiled much later by:

### Imam Abū Zakariyyā Yaḥyā ibn Sharaf an-Nawawī

**631–676 AH / 1233–1277 CE**

Imam an-Nawawi selected narrations dealing with:

* Sincerity
* Repentance
* Patience
* Taqwā
* Worship
* Good character
* Manners
* Family relationships
* Social behavior
* Knowledge
* Dhikr
* Duʿā
* Other aspects of righteous living

He organized them **thematically**, rather than simply arranging them according to narrator or chain.

Many of his selected hadith come from **Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim**, while others come from the Sunan and other collections.

Therefore:

> **Riyāḍ as-Sāliḥīn is a thematic collection of hadith for practical Islamic life; it is not itself a “Ṣaḥīḥ” collection in the same sense as Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī or Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim.**

# 11. The Most Important Concept

Hadith preservation should not be understood simply as:

> **“Someone wrote a book hundreds of years later.”**

A more accurate picture is:

> **Prophet ﷺ → Companions → Tābiʿūn → succeeding generations → chains of transmission → written records and collections → critical examination of narrators and texts → systematic hadith books and scholarship.**

The written collections are therefore **the later organized form of a much older transmission tradition** involving memorization, teaching, writing, isnād, travel, comparison and critical evaluation.

---

# 12. One-Line Summary

> **Ṣaḥīḥ** = authentic and strongest accepted classification
> **Ḥasan** = good and acceptable, but below Ṣaḥīḥ
> **Ḍaʿīf** = weak and not established as authentic
> **Mawḍūʿ** = fabricated and falsely attributed to the Prophet ﷺ

And historically:

> **The Sunnah was transmitted through generations by memorization, practice, writing and isnād, while Muslim scholars gradually developed a detailed science for verifying narrators, chains and texts and eventually compiled the major hadith collections.**
