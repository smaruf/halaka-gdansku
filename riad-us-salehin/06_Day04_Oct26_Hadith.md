## Hadith of the day: -->
> عن أبي هريرة رضي الله عنه قَالَ: قَالَ رَسُول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم: ((مِنْ حُسْنِ إسْلامِ المَرْءِ تَرْكُهُ مَا لا يَعْنِيهِ)). حديث حسن. رواه الترمذي وغيرُه. 
هذا الحديث أصل عظيمٌ من أصول الأدب. 
قيل للقمان: ما بلغ بك ما نرى؟ قال: صِدْقُ الحديث، وأداء الأمانة، وترْكُ ما لا يعنيني. 
وقال سهل بن عبد الله التُّسْتَري: مَن تكلَّم فيما لا يعنيه حُرم الصدق. ورُوي عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم أنه قال: ((أول من يدخل عليكم رجل من أهل الجنة))، فدخل عليهم عبد الله بن سلام، فقام إليه ناسٌ فأخبروه، وقالوا له: أخبرنا بأوثق عملك في نفسك؟ قال: إن عملي لضعيف، وأوثق ما أرجو به سلامة الصدر، وتركي ما لا يعنيني. 
قال الغزالي: حدُّ ما لا يعنيك في الكلام: أنْ تتكلَّم بما لو سَكَتَّ عنه لم تأثم، ولم تتضرر حالًا ولا مآلًا.

---

### English: -->

Abu Hurayrah رضي الله عنه reported that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said:

> **“Part of the excellence of a person’s Islam is that he leaves alone what does not concern him.”**

This is a **good (ḥasan) hadith**, narrated by al-Tirmidhi and others.

This hadith is considered one of the **great foundations of proper manners and conduct**.

It was said to Luqmān: *“What has brought you to the position we see you in?”* He replied:

> **“Truthfulness in speech, fulfilling the trust, and leaving what does not concern me.”**

Sahl ibn ʿAbdullāh al-Tustarī said:

> **“Whoever speaks about what does not concern him will be deprived of truthfulness.”**

It has also been narrated that the Prophet ﷺ said:

> **“The first person to enter upon you will be a man from the people of Paradise.”**

Then ʿAbdullāh ibn Salām entered. Some people approached him, informed him of what had been said, and asked:

*“Tell us about the deed you have the greatest confidence in.”*

He replied:

> **“My deeds are weak, but the strongest thing I hope will bring me safety is having a sound heart and leaving what does not concern me.”**

Al-Ghazālī said:

> **“The definition of what does not concern you, regarding speech, is that you say something which, had you remained silent about it, you would neither have sinned nor suffered any harm, either immediately or eventually.”**

**Main lesson:** A good Muslim learns to focus on what genuinely concerns him—his faith, responsibilities, actions, and character—and avoids unnecessary speech, curiosity, interference, and involvement in matters that bring no real benefit.
